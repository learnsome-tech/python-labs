## Goal

You can build conditions with and, or and not, read their precedence and short circuit behaviour, and return a Boolean expression directly instead of wrapping it in an if else.

In the lesson: The third operator is not, and it does what the English word does: it turns a true condition false and a false condition true. To ask whether a point is not inside a rectangle, you write not in front of the test. Then there is the question of what binds to what. The comparisons all bind tighter than the three Boolean operators, which is why you can write comparisons either side of an and without brackets. Among the three themselves, not binds tightest, then and, then or. When an expression gets long, put brackets in for the reader anyway.
